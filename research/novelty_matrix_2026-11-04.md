## Candidate distinction, not a priority claim

Test the **interaction between information actually available in context and assigned clarification policy**, with direct human perceived-intelligence ratings in live speech, separate independently assessed success and user repair burden, and prospective prediction on held-out provider families. The closest inspected studies establish pieces of this combination. None of the inspected sections establishes this entire proposed design, but this bounded search does not prove worldwide firstness.

Zheng's primary experiment uses prepared response paragraphs, questions about unspecified preferences and planted constraint violations; its reported measures include alignment, trust and satisfaction. Our proposed contrast concerns the status of the requested information before the question, with direct intelligence as a separate endpoint. This distinction must survive full review and successful measurement; swapping the endpoint label alone is insufficient.

AskToAct explicitly limits any claim that “redundant clarification” is a newly discovered failure category. The possible contribution is its experimentally identified human perceptual cost under context changes and transfer, not the category's existence. Human-vs-model comparisons in Madge likewise mean the observational phase is methodological grounding rather than an independently novel contribution.

## Decision rules before preregistration

1. Search the exact contrast and backwards/forwards citations of the nearest papers. Include conversational grounding, repeated requests, redundant questions, memory failures, repair burden, partner models and voice/text modality.
2. Extract population, modality, manipulated factors, exact outcome items, observation unit, control of final content, and holdout scheme. Abstract-only access cannot clear a novelty threat.
3. If a close duplicate exists, explicitly frame as replication/transport or change the mechanism. More brands or a Russian translation does not automatically justify a priority claim.
4. Do not use synthetic users or LLM judges to claim perceived-intelligence validation. A predictive increment and a causal effect are different contributions.
