# Provenance and scope of real human data

Retrieval date: **4 November 2026**. These are existing studies, not new participants recruited by this project. No synthetic participant ratings were generated. Original data are kept under `data/raw`; derived tables are reproducibly generated separately. `data/download_manifest.json` pins the nine primary data/material files with byte counts, URLs and SHA256. Run `python scripts/download_data.py --verify-only` to verify local originals or omit the flag to acquire missing files and regenerate SPSS CSV exports.

Audit correction: both modes now regenerate the consumed SPSS CSV exports after
all raw hashes pass. `--verify-only` means no downloads, not trust in an existing
derived CSV. Historical metadata/codebook files mentioned below are not all
regenerated by the downloader; use the original SAV metadata and linked source
materials when independently verifying those statements.

## Primary voice-perception evidence: Sarigül, Schneider and Utz (2026)

- Article: *Beyond voice: how do social role and speech style affect the perceived credibility and intelligence of voice assistants?* Review of Communication, publication online 6 March 2026. https://doi.org/10.1080/15358593.2026.2621084
- Author repository: https://osf.io/exmc8/; metadata saved in `data/raw/beyond_voice/node_metadata.json`.
- Files: `experiment1.sav` (353 rows), `experiment2.sav` (200 rows), `scale_items.pdf`, two preregistration PDFs. The article describes 216 recruited in Experiment 2, 200 retained after technical exclusions; the released cleaned file has 200, not 216. CSV files are mechanical exports from SPSS; codebook JSONs preserve variable/value labels.
- Participants: UK adult native-English-speaking Prolific participants watched video vignettes of voice-assistant interactions. They rated actual supplied stimuli; they did **not** themselves conduct live voice interactions. Experiment 1 manipulated servant/partner social role and assertive/affiliative style; Experiment 2 manipulated role with assertive style held constant.
- Outcomes: five explicit perceived-intelligence items and five credibility items, plus user covariates and manipulation checks. Never substitute credibility for intelligence. Use codebooks and scale PDF for direction and exact field names. Failed manipulation checks are outcomes of exposure; dropping those participants changes the randomized estimand. All released randomized cases should remain primary; selected samples are sensitivity analyses only.
- Rights: public readable OSF files; **no explicit repository license was present in node metadata**. The deposited consent text allows anonymous scientific use, including research outputs. Public access and participant consent do not establish a blanket redistribution license. Preserve attribution; do not label these files CC-BY or include them under the analysis code's license. For public release of a replication package, distribute the downloader/checksums and derived aggregate results; seek source clarification before bundling unrestricted raw-data copies.
- Limit: this is stimulus-based perception, not objective task performance. A model fit here cannot identify latency or task-success effects unavailable in the data.

## Direct human–AI interaction and intelligence items: Komura and Yamada (2026)

- Article: *Deepening ideas vs. exploring new ones: AI strategy effects in human-AI creative collaboration*. PLOS ONE 21(1):e0340449, 7 January 2026. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0340449
- Primary complete dataset: Supplement S3, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0340449.s003; local `data/raw/komura2026/s003.xlsx`.
- Questionnaire/materials: Supplement S2, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0340449.s002; `s002.docx`, with extracted `questionnaire.txt` for inspection. Downloaded primary full text `paper.html`.
- Participants: 148 real human participants interacting turn by turn with GPT-4.1 in a visual brainstorming canvas, generating café-sales ideas. **Text/canvas modality, not voice.** Three designed AI strategies: horizontal, vertical, random. The raw file actually has 52 vertical, 50 horizontal, 40 random, and **6 unknown-condition** sessions (P054, P001, P053, P002, P107, P108). All 148 are usable for measurement analyses; condition-based primary prediction uses 142 known assignments and retains all 148 as a labeled sensitivity analysis. The published study recruited through Yahoo Crowdsourcing; responses include Japanese text.
- Workbook `questionnaires`: 148 participant/session rows and 45 fields, including `sessionId`, `aitype`, age, gender, strategy perception, free response, adapted Godspeed and MDMT items.
- `sessions_metrics`: 148 rows, session duration in seconds, selected AI/user/total note counts, maximum grid coordinates. Duration is total session duration, **not response latency**. Selected ideas measure adoption, **not independently judged correctness or objective task success**.
- `notes_detailed`: 5,849 note rows, linked through `sessionId`, with creator, content, turn, selection, grid position. Participants could delete/overwrite their own notes, so denominator is the observed surviving AI/user notes. The release contains exactly 20 AI notes in every session; `ai_notes` is therefore a constant and must not be interpreted as a predictor. Source text reports 5,848 total notes but the worksheet contains **5,849**. `note_id` is not unique after anonymization (73 within-session duplicate IDs); `(sessionId, creator, turnId)` is unique. Session-key sets match across sheets and selected-note counts reconcile exactly with detailed records.
- Crucial instrument detail from S2: the five columns named `gqs_perceived_intelligence_1`…`_5` represent Incompetent–Competent, Unresponsive–Responsive, Ignorant–Knowledgeable, Irresponsible–Responsible, Unintelligent–Intelligent. This differs from the usual published Godspeed intelligence item set. Treat it as an **adapted scale**, not strict measurement invariance with other studies. All five are positive in the increasing direction. MDMT items use 0–7; Godspeed items use 1–5. Safety items 2 and 3 require reversal. S2's likeability block also contains an unusual duplicated adjective pair and a fear item; this further limits exact cross-study equivalence.
- Rights: publisher article and supporting materials are distributed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); credit Komura and Yamada and mark modifications. Original files were not altered.

## Randomized latency and task evidence: Tan et al. (2026)

- Article: *The Impact of Response Latency and Task Type on Human-LLM Interaction and Perception*. CHI 2026. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790716; full text https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06183v1.
- Authors: Felicia Fang-Yi Tan, Moritz A. Messerschmidt, Wen Yin, Oded Nov.
- Primary repository: https://osf.io/367cw/. `data/raw/tan2026/DATASET.csv` from https://osf.io/download/4bxvc/; `README.txt` from https://osf.io/download/q4gac/. OSF file manifests, node metadata and license object retained. The release is already anonymized/processed by its authors.
- **2,651 logged events from 240 participants**, not 2,651 independent users. Seven event classes: new-generation (1,376), copy (857), paste (336), new-chat (35), re-generation (23), edit-generation (13), cut (11). Participant surveys occur once per participant; other event-row survey cells are deliberately blank. Never interpret those blanks as attrition or impute the repeated records into independent outcomes.
- Design: 3 randomized time-to-first-token delays (2, 9, 20 seconds) × 2 task types (Creation, Advice), between participants; each participant completes three task instances of their assigned type. Streaming rate fixed in the original protocol. **Text chat, not voice.** The released file contains assigned delay, not measured request-level latency.
- Seven subjective response-quality items (1–7): clarity, relevance, understanding, thoughtfulness, usefulness, trustworthiness, expectation-gap. The latter is positively worded as meeting expectations; it is not a signed prediction error. Six NASA-TLX items use 0–100. `latency_perception` is post-treatment yes/no. No direct intelligence item, objective correctness, response text, demographic covariates or acoustic measures appear in this release.
- Rights: OSF license metadata explicitly identifies **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International**; full object in `data/raw/tan2026/license.json` (license ID 563c1cf88c5e4a3877f9e96a). Credit authors and distinguish our secondary analysis from their published analysis.
- Data audit: three IDs (`P132`, `P186`, `P215`) carry both Advice and Creation event rows despite the stated between-participant design. Delay is internally consistent and each ID has one survey record. The primary n=240 analysis uses the task label attached to that unique survey record; a transparent sensitivity analysis excludes the three ambiguous IDs (n=237). Predictive models use the unambiguous n=237 sample because event aggregation is otherwise uncertain. This is an unresolved source-data ambiguity, not evidence of actual participant crossover.
- Analysis unit: aggregate event counts to participant, join the one survey row, and fit randomized effects without conditioning on post-treatment counts or perceptions. Counts may be used in a separate predictive analysis, not as baseline causal covariates.

## Why not use other tempting datasets as direct intelligence evidence?

- [USS](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/user-satisfaction-simulation), Sun et al. (SIGIR 2021), supplies 6,800 dialogues across human commerce, Wizard-of-Oz and recommendation datasets with externally annotated satisfaction. Useful for satisfaction modeling but lacks direct perceived-intelligence and voice labels; no generic substitution is valid. Inspected repository/schema, not used in the final empirical models.
- [FED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12719), Mehri and Eskenazi (2020), provides fine-grained crowd evaluation of dialogue. Quality dimensions are not an intelligence measurement instrument; old text systems would not alone satisfy the requested voice/intelligence claim. Not acquired for analysis.
- [VoiceAssistant-400K](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gpt-omni/VoiceAssistant-400K) is audio/text training material, not real human subjective ratings. Excluded.
- [Fair-speech](https://ai.meta.com/datasets/speech-fairness-dataset/) contains real speakers and demographics but evaluates ASR, not perceived intelligence. Excluded.
- Shiramizu et al. (2022), [synthetic voice perceptions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-27124-8), has genuine human intelligence/competence ratings and public [OSF](https://osf.io/4zgrf/) data. Useful supporting literature, but does not jointly measure interactive task performance. Repository inspected, not pooled with the 2026 studies. An early manifest was downloaded while scouting; it is not a primary analyzed dataset.

## Transport limits and completion boundary

The three source studies answer complementary questions but have different constructs, modalities, tasks, scales and participants. A cross-source synthesis is not a single pooled sample with a common validated latent metric. No source jointly observes natural voice interactions, objective correctness/success, latency, error recovery, stable user traits and directly measured intelligence. Therefore the requested full causal/transportable model remains a research target; this package can deliver reproducible secondary empirical results and falsifiable claims, not pretend that missing measurements or a new user study exist.
